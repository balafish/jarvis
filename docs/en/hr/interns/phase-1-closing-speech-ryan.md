### ▍Slide 2 — What You Actually Built

> Let me tell you what I see from the outside.
>
> When you joined, Jarvis Hire was mostly a plan. A deck. An idea we believed in.
>
> Two months later, it has real channels, real candidates, and real companies who know our name. **[pause]** One hundred companies contacted. Two social channels built from nothing. And every single task assigned to the three of you was completed — one hundred percent, all summer.
>
> I want to be very clear about one thing. **[slow down]** This was not an exercise. We did not give you practice work while the adults did the real thing. You worked on the real product.
>
> In a small team, there is nowhere to hide. And there is also nowhere for good work to hide. I saw yours.

<details>
<summary>中文對照</summary>

讓我告訴你們，從外面看，我看到了什麼。

你們加入的時候，Jarvis Hire 大部分還只是一份計畫、一份簡報、一個我們相信的想法。

兩個月後，它有了真實的渠道、真實的人選，還有真的認得我們名字的企業。100 家企業被接觸、2 個社群渠道從無到有、而指派給你們三個人的每一項任務都完成了——整個夏天，100%。

有一件事我想說清楚：這不是實習作業。我們沒有讓你們做練習題，然後由大人去做真正的事。你們做的就是真實的產品。

在一個小團隊裡，沒有地方可以躲。同樣的，好的工作也藏不住。我看見了你們的。
</details>

### ▍Slide 3 — Nguyen Phuong Lam

> **Lam.**
>
> You came in as a marketing person. Within weeks, you were doing talent work too — reaching out to HR leaders on LinkedIn, building the framework of what Chinese-language hiring actually costs a company.
>
> Nobody asked you to cross that line. **[pause]** You just walked over it.
>
> That instinct — to pick up the thing that needs doing, before anyone hands it to you — is rare. It will carry you a long way.

<details>
<summary>中文對照</summary>

Lam。你是以行銷的身分進來的。幾週之內，你也開始做人才招募的工作——在 LinkedIn 上主動接觸 HR 主管，建立起「中文招募對一家企業到底代價多少」的框架。

沒有人要求你跨過那條線。你就自己走過去了。

那種直覺——在別人交辦之前，就先接下該做的事——很稀有。它會帶你走得很遠。
</details>

### ▍Slide 4 — Pham Thuy Duong

> **Duong.**
>
> You started two channels from absolute zero. No audience. No template. Nobody to copy.
>
> And you got posts to travel. Real people found us because of words you wrote.
>
> You told us you are still learning, and I respect that honesty. **[pause]** But look at what you did *while* learning. Starting from nothing is the hardest thing in marketing — and you did it twice.

<details>
<summary>中文對照</summary>

Duong。你從絕對的零開始，做起兩個渠道。沒有受眾、沒有範本、沒有可以抄的對象。

而你讓貼文傳出去了。真實的人因為你寫的文字找到我們。

你說你還在學習，我尊重這份誠實。但看看你在「還在學習」的同時做到了什麼。從零開始是行銷裡最難的事——而你做了兩次。
</details>

### ▍Slide 5 — Hoang Gia Minh

> **Minh.**
>
> One hundred companies in two months. You built our CRM from nothing. You finished the sales deck.
>
> And then you did the part most people skip — **[pause]** you brought the market back to us. The rejections. The reasons. What customers actually said.
>
> That feedback changed our product. Not everyone who sells, listens. You listened — and it made Jarvis Hire better.

<details>
<summary>中文對照</summary>

Minh。兩個月，100 家企業。你從零建起我們的 CRM，你完成了 sales deck。

然後你做了大多數人會略過的那一步——你把市場帶回來給我們。那些拒絕、那些理由、客戶真正說了什麼。

那些回饋改變了我們的產品。不是每個做銷售的人都會聽。你聽了——而這讓 Jarvis Hire 變得更好。
</details>

### ▍Slide 6 — AI-Native by Practice, Not by Title

> Now, the one thing I most want you to take with you.
>
> I have been building software for fourteen years. I have hired a lot of people. **[pause]** And here is what I noticed about the three of you.
>
> You didn't study AI. You *worked* with it. Every day. In your outreach, in your reports, in your analysis. For you, that is simply how work gets done.
>
> Most people your age have *used* AI. Very few have worked alongside it — on a real product, with real deadlines, for two months. **[slow down]** That is not a line on your CV. That is a way of working, and you already have it.
>
> You are AI-native by practice, not by title. Wherever you go next, that is yours. Nobody can take it back.

<details>
<summary>中文對照</summary>

接下來，是我最想讓你們帶走的一件事。

我做軟體十四年了，我面試過很多人。而關於你們三個，我注意到的是——

你們不是「學」AI。你們是「用」AI 在工作。每一天。在客戶開發裡、在報告裡、在分析裡。對你們來說，那就是工作本來的樣子。

跟你們同齡的人，大多「用過」AI。但很少有人是在真實的產品上、有真實的死線、連續兩個月，與它並肩工作。那不是履歷上的一行字，那是一種工作方式——而你們已經擁有了。

你們是靠實作而不是靠頭銜的 AI 原生世代。不論你們接下來去哪裡，那是你們的。沒有人拿得走。
</details>

### ▍Slide 7 — Closing

> At Jarvis, we say something to each other: **[pause]** we are never afraid that you fly too high. We are only afraid that you don't dare to fly.
>
> Nobody asked you to be perfect this summer. We asked you to be brave. **[pause]** You were.
>
> Thank you — for your energy, your questions, and your work. Whatever comes next for each of you, **this door stays open.**
>
> Kim, back to you.

<details>
<summary>中文對照</summary>

在 Jarvis，我們彼此會說一句話：我們從不怕你飛得太高，我們只怕你不敢飛。

這個夏天，沒有人要求你們完美。我們要求你們勇敢。而你們做到了。

謝謝你們——謝謝你們的活力、你們的提問、你們的付出。不論接下來各自往哪裡去，這扇門一直是開的。

Kim，交給你。
</details>

## 排練與現場提示

### 線上出席

| 項目 | 提示 |
|------|------|
| 語速 | 比平常再慢 15%。線上有延遲，快講會糊掉 |
| 停頓 | `[pause]` 處實停 1 秒。線上停頓感覺比實體短，要刻意拉長 |
| 鏡頭 | 講到三人名字時看鏡頭，不要看螢幕上的臉 |
| 收音 | 用耳機麥克風，避免筆電內建麥克風的回音 |
| 投影片 | 建議讓 Kim 或 Nam 代為翻頁，你專心講 |
| 開場 | 提早 5 分鐘進線，確認畫面與聲音 |

### 三條紅線（現場臨場發揮時也要守住）

1. ❌ 不提轉正、offer、續聘 — 評核結論是延長實習
2. ❌ 不提分數、不做排名 — 三人分數接近
3. ❌ 不提任何待改進項目 — 留給一對一

### 若時間不夠

優先刪 **Slide 2 第二段**（"I want to be very clear…" 整段），可省約 20 秒，不影響整體結構。

### 若被追問「他們接下來會怎樣」

安全回答：
> "That's a conversation Nam, Kim and each of them will have together. What I can say today is that the work earned the next chapter."
